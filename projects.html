Real-Time Renewable Energy Data Platform

The Challenge: Wind and solar output isn't constant: it swings with the weather. Without visibility into those swings, operators can't proactively adjust operations, schedule maintenance, or balance load across sites, leaving renewable capacity underutilized.

What I Built: This platform monitors three wind farms and three solar farms in real time, pulling live weather data (wind speed, solar irradiance, temperature) to predict expected energy output at each site. By correlating weather patterns with production data, it surfaces insights into upcoming high- or low-production windows so operators can make informed decisions rather than finding out after the fact. I owned the on-premise architecture (driving Spark-transformed data through automated SSIS packages into a SQL Server data warehouse) and was responsible for the Solar Azure pipeline.

NYC TLC Taxi Trip Analytics Pipeline

The Challenge: Ride-hailing platforms like Uber and inDrive lose efficiency when drivers are idle in low-demand areas while riders in high-demand zones wait longer than they should. Without visibility into historical trip patterns, driver positioning is reactive; dispatch happens after demand is already there instead of ahead of it.

What I Built: This pipeline processes NYC TLC taxi trip data (Jan–Mar 2025) through Docker-staged PySpark ETL into a Hive warehouse, surfacing KPIs on trip volume, pickup/dropoff hotspots, and time-of-day demand through an interactive Python & Flask dashboard. It gives ride-hailing operators the data to proactively position drivers where and when demand is about to spike, rather than finding out after riders are already waiting.

relational data cleaning & analytics

AdventureWorks Sales ETL & Analysis

The Challenge: Raw transactional data sitting in a relational database isn't usable for decision-making on its own: it's normalized across dozens of tables, full of inconsistencies, and has no narrative. Without a structured process to extract, clean, and shape it, sales and product trends buried in the AdventureWorks database stay invisible to anyone who isn't a SQL expert.

What I Built: My first end-to-end ETL pipeline: extracting data directly from the AdventureWorks relational database into Python, loading it into pandas DataFrames, and running it through extensive cleaning (handling nulls, inconsistent types, and merging across related tables) to get it analysis-ready. From there, I built visualizations surfacing sales and product trends that the raw database couldn't tell you on its own. It's a smaller-scope project than my later pipelines, but it's where I first learned what "clean data" actually requires.
